# Incident investigation: Hospital appointment booking intermittently returns 500s

## Summary

The booking-api is experiencing intermittent 500 errors, which users report as occurring in approximately one in ten requests. The issues are isolated to node api-2, which is currently failing to authenticate against the identity provider with a 403 Forbidden response when attempting to refresh its JWKS cache. Other nodes are functioning correctly, and the incident has resulted in at least one instance of a lost patient booking and duplicate confirmation texts.

*Directly supported by the input data.*

### Post hoc fallacy (`post\_hoc\_fallacy`)

* **Where it appears:** Hypothesis 1: 'The egress firewall policy (NET-2201) applied on 2024-10-28 likely uses an allow-list... that excludes the IP assigned to api-2 after its rebuild on 2024-11-01.'
* **Why it matters:** It assumes the firewall change on 2024-10-28 is responsible for the failure on 2024-11-01, but the rebuild of api-2 occurred later. The mechanism assumes the IP was excluded, but does not account for whether the firewall policy is IP-based or host-identity based.
* **Mitigation:** Verify the specific technical implementation of the egress firewall (e.g., is it based on IP address, service identity, or subnet?) before concluding the policy change is the cause.

### Availability bias (`availability\_bias`)

* **Where it appears:** Hypothesis 2: 'The rebuild of api-2 on 2024-11-01 may have resulted in a local environment where the base image contains an outdated or incorrectly initialized JWKS cache.'
* **Why it matters:** This suggests a common 'bad cache' explanation without evidence that the cache mechanism would retain data across a full image rebuild.
* **Mitigation:** Check the persistence configuration for the JWKS cache directory on the nodes to see if it actually survives a rebuild or image deployment.

### Overconfidence bias (`overconfidence\_bias`)

* **Where it appears:** Hypothesis 1 is assigned 'medium' confidence despite the fact that the actual firewall policy rules were not provided in the evidence.
* **Why it matters:** Assigning medium confidence to an untested assumption can cause an engineer to overlook other possibilities, such as an identity provider-side block of that specific node.
* **Mitigation:** Re-rank all hypotheses to 'low' until the network allow-list contents are audited.
